Gear vehicle & driver

A crew may send a truck, trailer or large vehicle with its gear. You provide your own driver. Without one, gear rides in the canoes, and that's fine.

  1. After launchLoad the gear and drive straight to tonight's campground.
  2. Hold the sitesRegister, pay cash, and set up a tent or dining fly on each site.
  3. MiddayDrive back upriver to the lunch spot and meet the crew.
  4. AfternoonRun into Grayling or Mio for ice, food and supplies.
  5. EveningMeet the crew at camp with the gear.

Take turns

No one needs to spend four days in the car. Rotate half-day shifts so every adult gets plenty of river time. Set the rotation before camp so everyone knows his shifts.

Driver carries

Campsite cash and the reimbursement form/envelope (if the crew leader isn't driving), copies of medical & permission forms, a first aid kit, a charged phone and charger, and the day's lunch spot and campground.

The crew's way off the river

If anyone is injured, sick or has an emergency, the driver helps get him off the river and to safety, whether that's the hospital in Grayling or resting in the vehicle. Two-adult plan when transporting a young man: TBD · Crews without a vehicle: emergency transport plan TBD

Keep everyone safe

Leader safety notes

The full river safety guide for families is on the main site. These are the leader-specific pieces.

Lead canoe

Experienced navigators who know every stop. Never passed. Signals every canoe in at each stop. Carries a map (required).

Last canoe

The most experienced paddlers, with at least one adult. No one ever behind it. Helps anyone stuck or tipped; calls for help if needed. Map recommended.

Loading canoes

1–2 per canoe is best. Waterproof every load. Clip in small items, but leave big bags loose. Sealed bags float, and tied-in gear makes a tipped canoe hard to right.

Food at night

Lock food in a vehicle if you can; otherwise use closed, secure containers. Crews without a vehicle: plan ahead (another crew's vehicle, lockable bins, or a bear hang). Plan TBD

Medical

Know every young man's medications. Crew first aid kit with extra athletic tape. Hospital in Grayling. If a young man must go home and there aren't enough leaders to drive, parents are asked to pick him up (~3 hrs).

Communication

Cell service at most campgrounds; a short drive usually finds it. At least one satellite communicator. Who carries it: TBD

Required for the lead canoe

A Map Guidebook to the Au Sable River

Each crew needs at least one copy, ideally two: one in the lead canoe (required) and one in the last canoe (recommended). Waterproof and tear-resistant, with river mileage, access points and campsites marked.

Reimbursable: one book per crew. Keep the receipt and submit it through the stake's online reimbursement process.
